From the beginning to today

A story in six milestones

Airbus was built around a European idea: bringing several countries and their expertise together to design aircraft capable of competing globally.

1972 · A300

The first Airbus takes flight

The A300B1 prototype made its first flight in Toulouse on 28 October 1972. At the time, choosing two engines for a widebody was bold: it aimed for efficient operation on short- and medium-haul routes. The A300 also laid the foundations for European industrial cooperation.

Key takeaway: the maiden flight was originally scheduled for the previous day, but fog delayed it by 24 hours.

Airbus historical source ↗
1987 · A320

A new way to fly

The A320 made its first flight on 22 February 1987. It helped popularise digital fly-by-wire controls on a commercial airliner, where fly-by-wire : pilot commands are transmitted as electrical signals and interpreted by computers, rather than through a conventional direct mechanical connection.

Key takeaway: this approach also helped Airbus develop a common cockpit philosophy across several families, making it easier for crews to transition between certain models.

History de l’A320 — Airbus ↗
1994 · A330

Le long-courrier bimoteur s’impose

Entering service in 1994, the A330 became a versatile platform for airlines. It can serve high-demand regional flights as well as intercontinental routes. The family later evolved with variants offering different capacities and ranges.

Key takeaway: the A330neo, launched in 2014, builds on the A330 platform, adding Rolls-Royce Trent 7000 engines and a modernised wing.

History de l’A330 — Airbus ↗
2005–2007 · A380

The double-deck giant

The A380 made its first flight in 2005 and entered commercial service in 2007. Its full-length double deck and four engines make it instantly recognisable. It was designed to carry large numbers of passengers between major airports.

Key takeaway: the project involved an international industrial network, with components manufactured in several European countries and transported to assembly sites.

Discover the A380 — Airbus ↗
2013–2018 · A220

A family born under another name

The aircraft now known as the A220 was initially developed by Bombardier as the C Series. It first flew in 2013; Airbus became the programme’s majority partner in 2018. It then joined the Airbus catalogue, designed particularly for the smaller-capacity single-aisle segment.

Key takeaway: the A220 is assembled in locations including Mirabel, Canada, and Mobile, United States.

A220 origins — Airbus ↗
2013–2015 · A350

Modern materials for ultra-long distances

The A350 is designed for long-haul routes. Its structure makes extensive use of composite materials, especially carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer, to combine strength with reduced weight. The family mainly comprises the A350-900 and A350-1000.

Key takeaway: the suffix “XWB”, used when the programme launched, stands for Extra Wide Body and referred to the aircraft’s extra-wide cabin.
